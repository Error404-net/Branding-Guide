## Strategy

### Overview

Error404 is the personal brand of an independent security practitioner, covering
a homelab, a public web presence, and a Microsoft 365 tenant. It is not a company
and does not sell anything.

The name is the HTTP status code for "not found." The brand treats that as a
joke it is in on, rather than a problem to apologize for: the thing you were
looking for is missing, and the missing thing is the point.

Its origin is the 90s warez and BBS scene — ANSI group logos, `.NFO` release
tables, phreaking, arcade cabinets. That is a real lineage, not a costume. The
work it fronts is modern security and infrastructure engineering; the surface it
wears is the era that produced that culture.

The problem it solves is narrow and honest: technical work spread across a lab,
a tenant, several social profiles, and a public site otherwise looks like five
unrelated people. This system makes it read as one.

### Positioning

**Category.** Personal technical brand. Lab, public site, and professional
presence for one practitioner.

**What it is not.** Not a startup. Not an agency. Not a product. Not a
publication. It has no customers, no roadmap, and no growth target, and copy
should never imply otherwise.

**Competitive landscape.** `404media.co` occupies adjacent name-space and a
deliberately opposite lane: minimalist, black-on-black, content-first tech
journalism with almost no ornamentation. Error404 runs maximalist, textured, and
arcade-bright. The distinction is intentional and load-bearing — the two must
never be mistakable for one another.

**Differentials.** A committed period aesthetic applied with real discipline: a
locked palette, a fixed meaning per accent, a primary mark built from real
astronomical data, and a two-mode treatment split that survives contact with
Microsoft Word.

**Territory.** Where security engineering meets the demoscene.

### Personality

**Archetype.** The Outlaw, in its craft register rather than its rebellion
register — scene-native, technically literal, unimpressed by polish for its own
sake.

**Attributes.** Precise. Textured. Self-aware. Unfashionable on purpose.
Legible under pressure.

**It is.** A practitioner's brand that knows exactly which decade it is quoting
and why.

**It is not.** Nostalgic for its own sake. Edgy. Anti-corporate posturing.
A skin over generic SaaS design.

### Promise

**Core commitments.**

1. The texture is never sanded off to make something easier to approve.
2. Anything instructional is written to be understood on first read.
3. Anything that leaves the building in a client's inbox is legible before it is
   expressive.

**Base message.** The aesthetic is a commitment, not a filter.

**Synthesizing phrase.** Scene texture, engineering discipline.

### Guardrails

**Tone summary.** Dry, exact, and in on the joke. Never salesy, never cute,
never self-congratulatory.

**Boundaries.**

- Never claim commercial scale, team size, or customers. There are none.
- Never present the brand as an organization or use "we" to mean a company.
- Never put real contact details into a template, a mockup, or this file.
  Placeholder tokens only.
- Never let period flavor override legibility on a surface someone has to read
  to do their job.

**Litmus test.** Would this still read as deliberate to someone who recognizes
the reference, *and* remain usable to someone who does not? If it fails either
half, it is wrong.

## Voice

### Identity

Error404 writes like a competent engineer explaining something they have
actually done — plainly, without hedging, and without decoration where
decoration would cost clarity.

**Essence.** Terminal-literate, not terminal-cosplay.

### Tagline & Slogans

**Primary (LOCKED).**

```
!ignore → return "404: Message not found"
```

It reads as a function return and winks at prompt-injection and AI-scanner
culture. It is the signature sign-off and is valid anywhere a tagline fits.
It is locked: do not reword, retranslate, or "modernize" it.

**Alternatives.** None are approved. The primary tagline stands alone.

### Message Pillars

1. **Real sources, not references.** The mark is plotted from actual Libra star
   positions and magnitudes. The palette is sampled from a real arcade
   reference. Nothing is an approximation of a thing it could have been.
2. **One meaning per signal.** Each accent color carries one fixed job across
   every surface. Consistency comes from restraint, not from volume.
3. **Two modes, no third.** Digital where the screen is yours; Print/Corporate
   where it is not. Every surface resolves to one of the two.
4. **Legible under substitution.** The system assumes fonts will fail to load,
   platforms will override the stack, and printers will drop color. It is built
   to survive all three.
5. **Plain language for anything instructional.** Flavor lives in the tagline
   and the texture, not in the documentation.

### Phrases

- "The texture is the brand."
- "Scene texture, engineering discipline."
- "If it's printed, it's monochrome."
- "One background, one foreground, four accents."
- "Real star positions, real magnitudes."
- "Terminal-literate, not terminal-cosplay."

### Tonal Rules

Instructional and reference copy follows **ASD-STE100 (Simplified Technical
English)**, borrowed for its rules rather than adopted as a certification:

1. One idea per sentence.
2. Short sentences. Aim under 25 words.
3. Active voice.
4. One term per concept — never vary a term for stylistic variety.
5. Concrete words over abstract ones.
6. Imperative for instructions.
7. No ambiguous pronouns.
8. State the rule, then the reason, in that order.

**Applies to:** template and placeholder copy, the brand guidelines, the public
site, README and NFO copy, UI microcopy, and this file.

**Does not apply to:** the tagline, flavor and easter-egg copy, and casual
internal notes. Those stay in voice even where that bends a rule above.

**Terminology (fixed — one term per concept):**

| Concept | Use | Never |
|---|---|---|
| The Libra graphic | **mark** | logo, icon, badge, glyph |
| `ERROR404.NET` lettering | **wordmark** | logotype, text logo |
| Mark and wordmark together | **lockup** | combo, full logo |
| A version of the mark | **variant** | colorway, cut, pull, flavor |
| The two treatment systems | **Digital mode**, **Print/Corporate mode** | print mode, color mode, corporate |
| The color system | **Arcade New Wave** | the palette, the theme, the scheme |

**We say / never say:**

| We say | Never say |
|---|---|
| "The palette is locked." | "The palette is finalized for now." |
| "Use the mark on a scrim." | "Try to keep the logo visible." |
| "Print/Corporate mode is monochrome." | "Corporate mode is more subtle." |
| "This is not decided yet." | "TBD" / "coming soon" |

**Identity boundaries.** Never write in first-person plural as a company.
Never address the brand's owner by name in published material — published copy
is impersonal or imperative.

## Visual

### Colors

The **Arcade New Wave** palette is **LOCKED**. It is sampled from an arcade
reference image, not generated. Each accent carries one fixed meaning on every
surface; do not reassign an accent to suit a layout.

| Role | Hex | Fixed meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Background | `#231451` | Primary surface |
| Deep background | `#170C38` | Page shell, outer gradient edge, Entra load fallback |
| Foreground | `#F4F1FF` | Body text, reversed marks |
| Accent — Blue | `#4DE1FF` | Links, primary actions, one glitch-offset layer |
| Accent — Pink | `#FF5FA2` | Secondary glitch-offset layer, highlights |
| Accent — Yellow | `#FFE156` | Taglines, callouts, CTAs |
| Accent — Mint | `#37F0A6` | Rules and dividers, status and success |

**Usage rules.**

- One background, one foreground, and at most two accents on any single surface.
  This palette gets noisy fast.
- `#231451` and `#170C38` are a background *pair*, not five accents plus one.
  `#170C38` is never used as an accent.
- Print/Corporate mode uses none of the above: pure black on white.
- Accessibility overrides brand. Where an accent fails contrast against its
  background at the required ratio, adjust the implementation and note the
  adjustment. Do not ship the failing combination.

### Typography

Five roles, one assigned face each, each with a declared fallback.

| Role | Face | Fallback | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display / ASCII wordmark | Custom block lettering | Press Start 2P | Hand-built artwork, not a font |
| Display fallback | Press Start 2P | `monospace` | Hero only. Unreadable below ~24px |
| Terminal accent | Perfect DOS VGA 437 | VT323 | Headers, code, NFO tables |
| Terminal web | VT323 | `monospace` | Web equivalent of the above |
| UI body | JetBrains Mono | `monospace` | Body copy. The only face on Entra and M365 surfaces |

**Rules.**

- Never more than two mono families on one surface.
- **Always declare a generic fallback.** A family named alone falls back to the
  browser default serif, not to monospace. This has shipped as a real defect.
- Entra ID and the Microsoft 365 admin center reject custom web fonts and
  substitute their own. That substitution is expected and needs no correction;
  specify JetBrains Mono anyway so the intent is recorded.

### Style

**Design keywords.** ANSI, NFO, glitch, chromatic aberration, DOS terminal, BBS,
phreak, arcade cabinet, CRT scanline, block character, teletext, dial-up.

**References.** ACiD and iCE-style ANSI group logos with block half-height
characters and drop-shadow banners; classic `.NFO` release tables with boxed
ASCII borders; VHS/CRT chromatic-split glitch text; 80s–90s arcade cabinet neon
geometry.

**Direction statement.** Maximalist, textured, arcade-bright — the deliberate
opposite of minimal content-first tech design.

**Avoid.** Corporate SaaS gradients. Soft rounded-corner cards. Inter or Roboto.
Emoji used as icons. Any "clean flat design" pass that sands off the CRT/ANSI
texture.

**Primary mark.** The Libra constellation mark (**LOCKED — selected**). A
node-link graph plotted from real Libra star positions and apparent magnitudes,
with `404` — or `4Ø4` in the ham variant — centered against a flat, fully opaque
halo. The halo is a solid plate, never a gradient or a glow.

- **Clear space:** the height of one `4` glyph on all sides.
- **Minimum size:** 24px for the flat reduced variant; 128px and up for the full
  texture.
- **Never:** stretch or skew it; recolor outside the palette; place it on a busy
  background without a scrim; use the multi-accent variant where only two colors
  are available.

**Treatment modes.**

| | Digital mode | Print/Corporate mode |
|---|---|---|
| Used for | Web, PowerPoint, social, merch, any screen you control | Word, PDF reports, letterhead, email, anything read in a client's inbox |
| Color | Full Arcade New Wave | Pure black on white |
| Mark | Full glitch/chromatic-offset lockup | Flat monochrome mark only |
| Identity carrier | Neon geometry, scanlines, glow | ASCII rules, boxed fields, bracket notation |

PowerPoint is the one documented exception: it is an export but stays in Digital
mode, because slides are a screen medium.
