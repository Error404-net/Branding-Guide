## Strategy

### Overview

Error404 is the personal brand of an independent security practitioner, covering
a homelab, a public web presence, and a Microsoft 365 tenant. It is not a company
and does not sell anything.

The name is the HTTP status code for "not found." The brand treats that as a
joke it is in on, rather than a problem to apologize for: the thing you were
looking for is missing, and the missing thing is the point.

Its origin is the 90s warez and BBS scene — ANSI group logos, `.NFO` release
tables, phreaking, arcade cabinets. That is a real lineage, not a costume. The
work it fronts is modern security and infrastructure engineering; the surface it
wears is the era that produced that culture.

The problem it solves is narrow and honest: technical work spread across a lab,
a tenant, several social profiles, and a public site otherwise looks like five
unrelated people. This system makes it read as one.

### Positioning

**Category.** Personal technical brand. Lab, public site, and professional
presence for one practitioner.

**What it is not.** Not a startup. Not an agency. Not a product. Not a
publication. It has no customers, no roadmap, and no growth target, and copy
should never imply otherwise.

**Distinctiveness.** The brand runs maximalist, textured, and arcade-bright.
That is a deliberate stance, not a default, and it is what keeps Error404 from
reading like the minimal black-on-black house style most technical work in this
space defaults to.

**Differentials.** A committed period aesthetic applied with real discipline: a
locked palette, a fixed meaning per accent, a primary mark built from real
astronomical data, and a two-mode treatment split that survives contact with
Microsoft Word.

**Territory.** Where security engineering meets the demoscene.

### Personality

**Archetype.** The Outlaw, in its craft register rather than its rebellion
register — scene-native, technically literal, unimpressed by polish for its own
sake.

**Attributes.** Precise. Textured. Self-aware. Unfashionable on purpose.
Legible under pressure.

**It is.** A practitioner's brand that knows exactly which decade it is quoting
and why.

**It is not.** Nostalgic for its own sake. Edgy. Anti-corporate posturing.
A skin over generic SaaS design.

### Promise

**Core commitments.**

1. The texture is never sanded off to make something easier to approve.
2. Anything instructional is written to be understood on first read.
3. Anything that leaves the building in a client's inbox is legible before it is
   expressive.

**Base message.** The aesthetic is a commitment, not a filter.

**Synthesizing phrase.** Scene texture, engineering discipline.

### Guardrails

**Tone summary.** Dry, exact, and in on the joke. Never salesy, never cute,
never self-congratulatory.

**Boundaries.**

- Never claim commercial scale, team size, or customers. There are none.
- Never present the brand as an organization or use "we" to mean a company.
- Never put real contact details into a template, a mockup, or this file.
  Placeholder tokens only.
- Never let period flavor override legibility on a surface someone has to read
  to do their job.

**Litmus test.** Would this still read as deliberate to someone who recognizes
the reference, *and* remain usable to someone who does not? If it fails either
half, it is wrong.

## Voice

### Origin

Error404 was Jesse's handle in the 90s, when he was a script kiddie. He took it
back when he found the domain and got back into cybersecurity. The warez scene
influenced the design choices here, and his repos use NFO-style READMEs.

The zero comes from his amateur radio licence and is woven into the HTTP 404
status code. The Libra scales give the mark its shape. AI tools helped him
build the palette and the font faster than working by hand would have allowed.

### Identity

Error404 writes like a competent engineer explaining something they have
actually done — plainly, without hedging, and without decoration where
decoration would cost clarity.

**Essence.** Terminal-literate, not terminal-cosplay.

### Tagline & Slogans

**Primary (LOCKED).**

```
!ignore → return "404: style not found"
```

It reads as a function return and winks at prompt-injection and AI-scanner
culture. Valid anywhere a tagline fits — site, marketing, the PDF guide.
It is locked: do not reword, retranslate, or "modernize" it.

**Exception: not the email signature.** The wink works for a human reading
the site; it doesn't work as well on outbound mail, where a "!ignore →" line
risks a false-positive from a recipient's own AI-based mail security
scanner on every message sent. The canonical signature format (see
`email-templates/`) omits it — sign off with the wordmark, not the tagline.

**Authorized variant — LinkedIn / network contexts.**

```
!ignore → return "404: Server not found"
```

Substitutes "Server" for "Message" to play on the platform metaphor — a professional network
is a server, not a mailbox. Same lock rules as the primary: do not reword it further. Same
signature exception applies — not used in outbound email.

### Message Pillars

1. **Real sources, not references.** The mark is plotted from actual Libra star
   positions and magnitudes. The palette is sampled from a real arcade
   reference. Nothing is an approximation of a thing it could have been.
2. **One meaning per signal.** Each accent color carries one fixed job across
   every surface. Consistency comes from restraint, not from volume.
3. **Two modes, no third.** Digital where a person is looking at a screen the owner
   controls; Machine mode where the sender is a machine or the medium is plain
   text. Every surface resolves to one of the two.
4. **Legible under substitution.** The system assumes fonts will fail to load,
   platforms will override the stack, and printers will drop color. It is built
   to survive all three.
5. **Plain language for anything instructional.** Flavor lives in the tagline
   and the texture, not in the documentation.

### Tonal Rules

Instructional and reference copy follows **ASD-STE100 (Simplified Technical
English)**, borrowed for its rules rather than adopted as a certification:

1. One idea per sentence.
2. Short sentences. Aim under 25 words.
3. Active voice.
4. One term per concept — never vary a term for stylistic variety.
5. Concrete words over abstract ones.
6. Imperative for instructions.
7. No ambiguous pronouns.
8. State the rule, then the reason, in that order.

**Applies to:** template and placeholder copy, the brand guidelines, the public
site, README and NFO copy, UI microcopy, and this file.

**Does not apply to:** the tagline, flavor and easter-egg copy, and casual
internal notes. Those stay in voice even where that bends a rule above.

**Terminology (fixed — one term per concept):**

| Concept | Use | Never |
|---|---|---|

| The mark with `4Ø4`, 128 px and up | **Logo** | full logo, primary logo |

| `ERROR404.NET` lettering | **wordmark** | logotype, text logo |
| Mark and wordmark together | **lockup** | combo |

| The two treatment systems | **Digital mode**, **Machine mode** | print mode, color mode, corporate mode, Print/Corporate |

**We say / never say:**

| We say | Never say |
|---|---|
| "The palette is locked." | "The palette is finalized for now." |

**Identity boundaries.** Never write in first-person plural as a company.
Never address the brand's owner by name in published material — published copy
is impersonal or imperative.

## Visual

### Colors

The **Arcade New Wave** palette is **LOCKED**. It is sampled from an arcade
reference image, not generated. Each accent carries one fixed meaning on every
surface; do not reassign an accent to suit a layout.

| Role | Hex | Fixed meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Background | `#231451` | Primary surface |
| Deep background | `#170C38` | Page shell, outer gradient edge, Entra load fallback |
| Foreground | `#F4F1FF` | Body text, reversed marks |
| Accent — Blue | `#4DE1FF` | Links, primary actions, one glitch-offset layer |
| Accent — Pink | `#FF5FA2` | Secondary glitch-offset layer, highlights |
| Accent — Yellow | `#FFE156` | Taglines, callouts, CTAs |
| Accent — Mint | `#37F0A6` | Rules and dividers, status and success |

**Neon accent set (display cuts only).** The neon-tube treatment of the mark glows
against a near-black backdrop, and the locked pastels do not glow. It borrows a
second, brighter set. These colors belong to the neon cuts of the mark and to
nothing else: not text, not UI, not links.

| Role | Hex | Used in |
|---|---|---|
| Neon green | `#39FF14` | `libra-neon-green`, single-accent green cuts |
| Neon cyan | `#00F0FF` | `libra-neon-blue` (not yet built) |
| Neon magenta | `#FF16E0` | `libra-neon-pink` (not yet built), `libra-neon-multi` |
| Neon backdrop | `#0B0810` | Baked into every neon cut |

`libra-neon-multi` also keeps locked mint `#37F0A6` and yellow `#FFE156`.

**Usage rules.**

- One background, one foreground, and at most two accents on any single surface.
  This palette gets noisy fast.
- `#231451` and `#170C38` are a background *pair*, not five accents plus one.
  `#170C38` is never used as an accent.
- Machine mode uses none of the above: black on white, nothing else.
- Say "purple" only about the background pair above. There is no separate brand
  purple; `#231451` is the whole story.
- Accessibility overrides brand. Where an accent fails contrast against its
  background at the required ratio, adjust the implementation and note the
  adjustment. Do not ship the failing combination.

### Typography

One house face sets all text, headings included. The Nerd Font build is for the terminal only. Each face has a declared web-safe fallback, because the system assumes fonts fail to load and must still read when they do.

| Role | Face | Fallback | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| House mono | Error404 Mono Regular and Bold | `Consolas, "Liberation Mono", "Courier New", monospace` | All text, headings, labels, and the `4Ø4` text in the mark |
| Terminal | Error404 Mono Nerd Font (PostScript `Error404MonoNF`) | the terminal's own monospace | Shell and prompt, with Nerd Font icons and the constellation OS icon |
| Wordmark | Hand-built block lettering | n/a | Artwork, not a font |
| Email and paper | Courier New | `Courier, monospace` | Email clients cannot load web fonts |
| Entra and M365 | Courier New | `monospace` | Microsoft replaces custom web fonts. Expected |

**Web-safe fallback stack** (use on every web surface):

```css
font-family: "Error404 Mono", Consolas, "Liberation Mono", "Courier New", monospace;
```

**Rules.**

- Error404 Mono ships in Regular and Bold. Do not fake a third weight: a synthetic bold smears the thin strokes.
  Show emphasis with colour, caps or size.
- The zero and Ø are drawn into the font. The Ø slash runs at 45.75°, the angle of the
  constellation edge, so the split line and the slash read as one stroke.
- Coverage today: ASCII, the arrows `← ↑ → ↓ ↔ ↕ ⇐ ⇒ ⇔`, and the icon glyph U+E404 (Nerd Font build). Box-drawing and block glyphs are pending.
- Never more than two mono families on one surface.
- **Always declare a generic fallback.** A family named alone falls back to the browser's
  default serif, not to monospace. This has shipped as a real defect.
- Licence: SIL OFL 1.1, derived from Share Tech Mono and JetBrains Mono. The font file is
  `font/Error404Mono-Regular.ttf`. Full licence texts are required before public distribution.

#### Terminal install (Powerlevel10k)

Nerd Font icons and the constellation OS icon.

**Live once published.** The Nerd Font builds are in `font/nerd/` on the font branch. The download path above
resolves only after that branch is published to the site. Do not share these steps before then.

```sh
# 1. Install the font (macOS)
for w in Regular Bold; do
  curl -fsSL -o ~/Library/Fonts/Error404MonoNerdFont-$w.ttf \
    https://branding.error404.net/font/nerd/Error404MonoNerdFont-$w.ttf
done
# Linux: use ~/.local/share/fonts/ instead, then run: fc-cache -f

# 2. Set the terminal font to Error404Mono Nerd Font. Do not set a separate non-ASCII font.
#    iTerm2:         Settings > Profiles > Text > Font. Untick "Use a different font for non-ASCII text"
#    Terminal.app:   Settings > Profiles > Font
#    GNOME Terminal: Preferences > Profile > Custom font
#    In a config file the PostScript name is Error404MonoNF, e.g. Error404MonoNF 15 in iTerm2.

# 3. Update ~/.p10k.zsh. Settings go inside the config body, not appended at the end:
#    anything after the final p10k reload is ignored.
cp ~/.p10k.zsh ~/.p10k.zsh.bak
perl -0pi -e '
  s/POWERLEVEL9K_MODE=\S+/POWERLEVEL9K_MODE=nerdfont-v3/;
  s/(^[ \t]*#[ \t]*typeset -g POWERLEVEL9K_OS_ICON_CONTENT_EXPANSION=.*\n)/$1  typeset -g POWERLEVEL9K_OS_ICON_CONTENT_EXPANSION=\$\x27\\uE404\x27\n/m;
' ~/.p10k.zsh
# Check that os_icon is in POWERLEVEL9K_LEFT_PROMPT_ELEMENTS. It is by default.

# 4. Reload
exec zsh
```

### Style

**Design keywords.** ANSI, NFO, glitch, chromatic aberration, DOS terminal, BBS,
phreak, arcade cabinet, CRT scanline, block character, teletext, dial-up.

**References.** ACiD and iCE-style ANSI group logos with block half-height
characters and drop-shadow banners; classic `.NFO` release tables with boxed
ASCII borders; VHS/CRT chromatic-split glitch text; 80s–90s arcade cabinet neon
geometry.

**Direction statement.** Maximalist, textured, arcade-bright — the deliberate
opposite of minimal content-first tech design.

**Avoid.** Corporate SaaS gradients. Soft rounded-corner cards. Inter or Roboto.
Emoji used as icons. Any "clean flat design" pass that sands off the CRT/ANSI
texture.

**Primary mark.** The Libra constellation mark (**LOCKED — selected**). A
node-link graph plotted from real Libra star positions and apparent magnitudes,
with `4Ø4` centered against a flat, fully opaque halo. The halo is a solid plate,
never a gradient or a glow.

- **Clear space:** the height of one `4` glyph on all sides.
- **Minimum size:** 24px for the flat reduced variant; 128px and up for the full
  texture.
- **Every export is transparent** except the four neon variants, which bake in a
  near-black backdrop because the glow only reads against a dark surface. Never
  place a mark on a flattened plate of its own — if a surface needs a plate,
  that is a decision the layout makes, not something baked into the asset.
- **Ring variants pair by surface.** The `white` and `multi-color` cuts draw the
  ring in `#F4F1FF` and belong on dark surfaces. The `black`, `green` and `pink` cuts draw
  it in near-black and belong on light ones. Putting a dark-ring cut on a dark
  background makes the ring disappear, which is easy to miss because the nodes
  and numerals still read. Every ring cut scales the constellation to 76.7% so all nodes sit inside the
  ring. The node coordinates themselves do not change.
- **No-ring variants** ship without the halo ellipse as of this revision. Use
  them for clean mark placement where the constellation lines and nodes alone
  carry the identity.
- **Lines are one uniform color per variant** — the multicolor cut draws every
  connecting line in Mint (`#37F0A6`); single-color cuts draw every line in
  that cut's own color. Node circles stay multicolor on the multicolor cut.
  Do not vary line color by edge except on the neon-glow variants, which keep
  their own per-edge glow treatment as a deliberate distinct aesthetic.
- **The line crossing the `4Ø4` numerals is two collinear segments, not one
  bent line.** The gap between them is sized and angled to exactly match the
  rendered diagonal slash inside the `Ø` glyph — measured from the glyph
  itself (edge detection + line fit), not eyeballed. Never rejoin it into a
  single line that ignores the glyph, and never bend it to a different angle.
- **Never:** stretch or skew it; recolor outside the palette; place it on a busy
  background without a scrim; use the multi-accent variant where only two colors
  are available.

**Treatment modes.**

| | Digital mode | Machine mode |
|---|---|---|
| Used for | Web, PowerPoint, social, merch, any screen you control | Automated and non-human senders: alerts, notifications, scheduled reports. Also paper and documents (Word, PDF, letterhead), which share the same constraint |
| Color | Full Arcade New Wave | Black on white. No color, no accent rule |
| Mark | Full glitch/chromatic-offset lockup | Flat monochrome mark only |
| Identity carrier | Neon geometry, scanlines, glow | ASCII rules, boxed fields, bracket notation |

PowerPoint is the one documented exception: it is an export but stays in Digital
mode, because slides are a screen medium.

**Why Machine mode exists.** Automated systems (monitoring, scanners, schedulers,
anything that sends mail without a person behind it) often cannot send rich HTML
email, and many recipients read mail as plain text anyway. Machine mode is how a
plain-text message still reads as Error404: ASCII rules, bracket fields, and
`::` / `~/` notation do the branding that color and a logo would do elsewhere.
A human-sent email signature is not Machine mode; it has its own light-touch
spec in `DESIGN.md`.

## Default mark (LOCKED)

**Construction.** The mark is an approximation of the Libra constellation, adjusted by hand.
It is not a plotted star chart. Several star layouts were combined into one mesh-network
graph, because the brand is a `.net` domain.

**Canonical default:** No-ring multicolor variant (constellation lines and nodes only, no halo).

Used for clean mark placement where the constellation geometry alone carries the identity. Works on both dark and light backgrounds with proper contrast. On dark backgrounds, use with full Arcade New Wave palette. On light backgrounds, pair with `libra-no-ring-black.svg`.

**Alternative default:** `4Ø4` in neon green (`#39FF14`) for single-color, canonical-choice contexts (site header, business card) on dark surfaces only.

**Logo and Icon (LOCKED).** There are two marks. The **Logo** has the `4Ø4` numerals and
starts at 128 px. The **Icon** has no numerals and no zero; it is for small and hard-to-see
places (favicons, app-icon slots, avatars under 128 px, tabs). Below 128 px, switch from Logo
to Icon. The minimum Icon size is 24 px (16 px for favicons only).

**Icon glyph (U+E404).** The same constellation as a single character in the Error404 Mono
Nerd Font build, added so the terminal prompt could be customised. It is a private-use code point,
clear of the Nerd Font ranges.

**Icon (LOCKED; formerly "Monogram").** Where no text fits (favicons,
app-icon slots, OBS overlays, anywhere the numerals would be too small to
read), use the **Icon**: constellation nodes and lines only, no `4Ø4`, no
halo, transparent background. Exactly two variants, named by the surface they
sit on rather than by color:

| Variant | Color | For | File |
|---|---|---|---|
| Night | White (`#F4F1FF`) | Dark surfaces — site favicon, dark-theme app icons, OBS overlays | `monogram-night.svg` |
| Daylight | Black (`#111111`) | Light surfaces — light-theme app icons, print | `monogram-daylight.svg` |

These two replace every prior icon-only or glyph-only cut (the old
`libra-site-icon-*` and `glyph-only/` files are deleted; see `CLAUDE.md`). Do not
introduce a third icon-only variant without updating this file.

## Mark build spec (LOCKED)

Every mark that carries the `4Ø4` text (primary, ring, neon) is built to this
spec. `scripts/verify-marks.py` checks it and must pass before a mark is
committed or re-rendered.

- **Geometry.** Six nodes and seven connecting lines at the canonical
  coordinates (viewBox `0 0 240 240`). Lines are `stroke-width="2"`,
  `opacity="0.75"`. Node circles keep their multicolor fills.
- **Line color.** Every line is one uniform color: mint `#37F0A6` on the
  multicolor mark, the mark's own solid color on the black, white and
  single-accent cuts. Neon cuts color each edge on purpose and are exempt.
- **Split line.** The edge from `(204.0, 74.9)` to `(80.2, 202.0)` crosses the
  `4Ø4` text, so it is drawn as two segments that both lie exactly on that
  edge (within 0.1 unit): `(204.0, 74.9)` to `(163.7, 116.3)` and
  `(125.8, 155.2)` to `(80.2, 202.0)`. Neon cuts use a wider gap for their
  thicker strokes.
- **Text.** `4Ø4` in Error404 Mono Regular (`font-weight="400"`; the mark never uses Bold), size 44 (40 on neon), `x="145"`, `y="151"`,
  `text-anchor="middle"`. No oval or ellipse behind it.
- **Verification.** Line ink and text ink are rendered as two separate masks
  with the real font. They must overlap by zero pixels and keep at least 1.5
  units of clearance. A visual check alone is not accepted; this has failed
  three times.
- **Measured angles.** The edge runs at 45.75°. The Ø slash in Error404 Mono Regular is drawn
  at the same angle (measured 45.63° by Canny edge detection plus Hough line fit on the isolated
  glyph), so the split line and the slash read as one stroke. The earlier Share Tech Mono
  (74.5°) and JetBrains Mono (60.5°) measurements are retired.
- **Render.** Use the real Error404 Mono Regular and grayscale antialiasing (see
  `CLAUDE.md`, working notes). A fallback font silently changes the text
  width and the Ø.
